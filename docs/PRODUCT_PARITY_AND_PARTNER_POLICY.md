**Status:** Product requirements baseline — approved direction from owner

This document is a product requirement for GiGo. It is not a claim that every module is already implemented. The assistant must eventually be able to invoke every enabled GiGo module through a governed tool registry and shared owner context.

## 1. Product objective

GiGo must cover the practical business-building workflow represented by Monetise and the referenced video, while becoming a broader, integrated personal AI operating system:

1. Discover a real problem and validate demand.
2. Choose a narrow audience and commercially useful offer.
3. Build a high-quality digital product or service.
4. Find and qualify distribution partners and customers.
5. Prepare personalised partnership proposals and outreach.
6. Launch and distribute through suitable channels.
7. Deliver the product/service and support customers.
8. Track revenue, costs, partner shares, conversion, and retention.
9. Learn from verified results and improve the next iteration.

GiGo should not merely reproduce a set of isolated prompt bots. Its differentiator is one assistant, one persistent database, reusable research evidence, connected business modules, durable jobs, approval gates, and an auditable end-to-end workflow.

## 3. Creator / partner qualification policy

The assistant must never choose a partner solely because of follower count. It must gather evidence, distinguish verified facts from estimates, score the candidate, explain risks, and recommend whether to shortlist, investigate further, or reject.

### 3.1 Four baseline filters reported in Iman Gadzhi's creator-partnership method

Treat these as initial screening heuristics, not universal laws or guarantees:

1. **Audience size:** roughly 10,000–100,000 followers for a micro-creator.
2. **Content reach:** example screening range around 50,000–200,000 views on relevant content. Confirm platform, time period and whether views are typical rather than one viral outlier.
3. **No existing competing product:** inspect bio links, pinned posts and recent content for an existing ebook, course, coaching, paid community or similar offer. Existing monetisation is not an automatic rejection if a complementary partnership is possible; flag it for review.
4. **Business contact route:** a publicly provided business email or clearly stated partnership contact. A visible email is not permission for indiscriminate bulk marketing.

These thresholds are source-specific starting points. Do not reject a highly relevant smaller creator or accept a weak-fit creator just because they match the numerical ranges.

### 3.2 Mandatory expanded due diligence

Before recommending a partner, assess and record:

- **Niche and audience fit:** audience problem, topic relevance, product need, and alignment between creator content and proposed outcome.
- **Audience authenticity:** suspicious follower spikes, repetitive/bot-like comments, engagement quality, suspicious geography mismatch, and unexplained view/follower inconsistencies.
- **Engagement quality:** meaningful comments, saves/shares where observable, recurring audience questions, creator replies, and typical performance across a sample of recent posts.
- **Consistency:** recent posting cadence, sustained reach, and whether performance depends on one viral post.
- **Audience evidence:** recurring pain points, questions, requests, objections and language drawn from public content; cite the specific posts/videos used.
- **Trust and reputation:** public controversies relevant to the offer, misleading claims, harmful conduct, undisclosed promotions, and product/customer complaints where evidence is available.
- **Commercial fit:** audience geography and purchasing power where lawfully and reliably observable; likely price fit; product-market fit; competing offers; launch channel.
- **Creator authority:** relevant experience, demonstrated skill, subject knowledge and consistency between claims and public work.
- **Brand and values fit:** tone, quality, safety, inclusion and compatibility with the proposed product.
- **Operational fit:** responsiveness, contact route, expected contribution, content rights, launch availability and willingness to agree written terms.
- **Legal and disclosure checks:** advertising disclosure, privacy, intellectual property, consumer protection, platform rules and applicable local laws.

### 3.3 Scoring and decision rules

Use a 100-point score, with evidence and confidence recorded for each category:

- Audience/problem/product fit: 20
- Audience authenticity and engagement quality: 20
- Creator credibility and reputation: 15
- Content reach and consistency: 10
- Commercial potential and unit economics: 15
- Brand/values fit: 10
- Operational readiness and contactability: 5
- Legal, disclosure and rights readiness: 5

Decision guidance:
- **80–100:** strong shortlist, subject to human review and unresolved-risk checks.
- **65–79:** investigate further; do not send a proposal automatically.
- **Below 65:** do not recommend at this stage.
- **Hard stop:** credible evidence of fraud, bought engagement, material dishonesty, serious brand-safety issue, unlawful contact, missing rights, or an unresolvable conflict. A high numeric score cannot override a hard stop.

Every score must show:
- evidence URL/post identifier and observation date;
- observed value versus estimated value;
- sample size/time window;
- confidence (high/medium/low);
- missing information and assumptions;
- reason for the recommendation.

Never invent private audience demographics or claim that an account's followers are real without adequate evidence.

### 3.4 Partnership economics

Before outreach, model conservative, base and optimistic scenarios. Show assumptions for:
- reachable audience (not total followers);
- expected impressions/views;
- click-through rate;
- conversion rate;
- refund/chargeback allowance;
- product price and platform/payment fees;
- fulfilment and support costs;
- creator/operator revenue split;
- taxes and other applicable costs.

Display gross revenue and estimated net proceeds separately. Do not present Iman Gadzhi's illustrative conversion or income examples as forecasts. No income or sales guarantee.

A partnership must have a written agreement covering deliverables, content and IP rights, approval process, disclosure, term, exclusivity if any, revenue definition, deductions, payment timing, refunds, access to reporting, termination and dispute handling. Require owner approval before accepting terms or publishing.

## 4. Assistant tool-use requirement

All modules must be exposed to the central assistant through a permissioned tool registry. The assistant should:
1. understand the user's goal and relevant project context;
2. choose the smallest suitable set of tools;
3. explain the intended plan for consequential workflows;
4. run research and draft work using stored evidence;
5. checkpoint long-running jobs and report progress;
6. request approval before sending outreach, spending money, signing/accepting terms, publishing, changing access, or other consequential external actions;
7. execute only the approved payload;
8. verify provider confirmation before reporting completion;
9. store outcomes, evidence and lessons in the shared database.

A module is not considered integrated merely because a page exists. It is integrated when the assistant can call its validated backend operation, retrieve its result, preserve context, and obey its permissions.

## 5. Safety, rights and provider boundaries

- Do not automate, scrape, or access Monetise's private software or member systems. GiGo must be independently implemented from general product requirements and public information.
- Monetise's current terms state that its included software is intended for manual use and prohibit bots/scripts/automated interaction with those tools. GiGo must not connect to or automate those products.
- Use permitted public sources and official APIs where available; respect robots rules, platform terms, privacy laws and rate limits.
- Public contact details are not blanket consent for bulk marketing. Track legal basis, source, purpose, opt-outs, suppression, bounces and complaints. Obtain owner approval before sending.
- Only use creator content to analyse publicly observable themes and prepare proposals. Do not copy protected content or impersonate a creator. Obtain licences/permission for derivative products, voice, likeness, photos, videos and brand assets.
- Keep all provider secrets server-side. Use least-privilege credentials and audit sensitive actions.
- Never fabricate evidence, testimonials, revenue, partner interest, audience demographics, product claims or provider success.

## 6. Current GiGo implementation comparison

Current implementation after the first workspace build:
- separate landing, login and private overview pages;
- Supabase authentication and owner account flow;
- initial Supabase schema for profiles, projects, tasks, conversations/messages, memory, jobs and audit events;
- Cloudflare Worker API and static assets;
- authenticated project and task API routes using the signed-in user's Supabase bearer token and Row Level Security;
- authenticated conversation, message and memory APIs, with user-owned rows and validation;
- a central assistant capability registry that distinguishes working tools from planned modules and marks external actions that will require approval;
- a protected capability-discovery endpoint for the signed-in owner;
- a database trigger that moves a conversation to the top of the list when a new message is saved;
- a GitHub Actions TypeScript-check workflow (the run covering the assistant API and UI passed; the latest runtime ownership fix is awaiting its own run).
- owner identity is verified server-side before inserting project/task rows;
- project and task create/list flows in the overview, task status updates, and live active-project/open-task counts;
- an additional database migration enforces that a task can only reference a project owned by the same account.

Not yet implemented/verified:
- end-to-end runtime verification of the new project/task flows after applying migration 0002 and deploying the latest Worker;
- an OpenAI-compatible AI chat runtime and mobile-friendly workspace chat UI are now implemented in code; provider credentials/model are not configured, and no live model request has yet been verified;\n- automatic model tool-calling/execution loop is not yet implemented: the current chat runtime receives workspace memory and capability context but cannot yet create/update projects or tasks through model-selected tools;
- research, product, partner, lead, CRM, offer, outreach, launch, store/payment, fulfilment, creative and analytics modules;
- partner scoring and evidence store;
- approval workflow UI and external action integrations;
- durable job execution/worker orchestration.

The next engineering sequence should establish secure database CRUD and shared assistant/tool foundations first, then deliver complete vertical workflows. Do not build disconnected placeholder pages and call them finished features.
